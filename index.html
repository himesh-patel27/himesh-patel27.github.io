01 / ABOUT ME

A little curiosity.
A lot of persistence.

I enjoy understanding how things work, figuring out why they stop working, and making technology easier for the person using it.

My interest in technology actually started with curiosity. I grew up playing computer games and wondering how they were made. I was just as curious about the computer itself: how it was built, how all the parts worked together, and whether I could learn to do the same.

That turned into something I still enjoy: treating a technical problem a little like a mystery. I like figuring out the difference between a quick fix that gets someone working again and a better long-term solution that addresses why it happened.

Retail has taught me to listen completely before jumping in. When someone knows very little about technology, I use what they tell me to troubleshoot, explain things in everyday language, and use examples or a bit of humour when it helps. I try to read the situation, too — someone having a difficult day needs a different approach.

More about how I work and what matters to me

I’ll try to work through a problem myself first, but being stuck is my cue to look at documentation, forums, YouTube tutorials, or ask someone with more experience. I’d rather learn than keep guessing. Mistakes in daily life and at work have shaped how I approach things the next time.

I’m proud of being reliable, especially when I can genuinely help someone. I’d like colleagues to know me as kind, funny, playful, and energetic. On busy or difficult days at SportChek, that can mean making sure part-time staff get their breaks on time, or occasionally ordering food for us to share.

IT is part of so much of everyday life, and security problems often reach ordinary people who don’t know what safe IT practices look like. That connection between systems and people is a big part of why I’m interested in both support and security.

Long term, I’d like to grow toward leadership and management, helping develop better ways to secure systems and improve IT infrastructure. I’m drawn to places where people feel valued, the atmosphere is relaxed and human, and everyone still takes responsibility for doing good work on time.

Once I set my eyes on something, I put real effort into making it work. For me, that means learning, asking questions, and trying again when the first approach doesn’t work.

04Digital Forensics Case Study+

WHAT I BUILT / INVESTIGATED

Investigated a simulated compromised Windows 7 CFO machine using FTK Imager, Volatility2, Registry Explorer, Event Log Explorer, and VirusTotal.

  • Identified Enhancement Tablxslx.exe, a malicious process disguised as Excel; 50/72 VirusTotal vendors flagged the file.
  • Found Mimikatz installed through PowerShell and Wdigest enabled, allowing plaintext credential dumping.
  • Reconstructed the timeline from phishing and malicious download through credential theft and remote access.
  • Produced seven remediation recommendations, including MFA and disabling Wdigest.

05Splunk Fraud Analysis+

WHAT I BUILT / INVESTIGATED

Built a 10-panel dashboard for the Commonwealth Bank Cybersecurity Job Simulation on Forage. This is a training artifact, completed in September 2026.

  • Used stats, top, eval, case(), and replace() to aggregate, clean, and visualize 200 simulated transactions.
  • Flagged 92/200 transactions as fraudulent; 84/92 fraud cases involved transportation.
  • Female customers had 49 fraudulent transactions versus 35 for male customers, overwhelmingly in transportation.
  • The 19–25 age group had the highest fraud count; 36/38 cases were directed at merchant M348934600.
  • Monthly fraud counts ranged from 19–27, with no strong seasonal trend in the simulated dataset.
